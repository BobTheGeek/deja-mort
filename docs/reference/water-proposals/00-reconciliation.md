## 1. The preferred solution — **COLD PAINT**

**COLD PAINT** keeps the water's existing geometry systems and turns the water into a *flat, self-lit painted surface with drawn white marks*: the puddle body becomes an unshaded, 0.85-alpha sheet whose source colour is pre-compensated for the frozen `post.saturation 0.55 / contrast 1.10` grade so the captured pixel lands on the mockup's own slate blue; the foam edge and four ripple rings become white unshaded bands carrying one generated **paint-brush texture** (bright core, soft halo, dashes) over lane 1's arc-length edge wobble; the cascade keeps its current boxes but gets one small **streak shader** that scrolls white foam down a pale-blue sheet and dissolves the box edges; and the droplets become **flat white marks** (a generated mark PNG on a horizontal particle quad) instead of shaded spheres. Everything is data in `content/visuals.json`, nothing touches the frozen room, no global frame effect is enabled, no new library, **$0**.

## 2. Why this one, and what each lane contributed

**Why.** The target is *not* a rendering problem, it is a colour-management problem plus a mark-making problem. All three lanes independently diagnosed the same root cause — a 45%-alpha lit blue sheet under an amber bulb, crushed by a 0.55 saturation grade — and all three prescribed the same core fix: **self-lit water, alpha 0.85, pre-compensated colour, near-white marks the grade cannot touch**. The lanes differ only in *mechanism*, and the honest ranking is: lane 3's mechanism is the cheapest and safest but cannot make painted marks; lane 2's mechanism makes the best marks but is the most expensive and largest surface area; lane 1 sits between. COLD PAINT takes the minimum mechanism for each of the six features from whichever lane's tool is actually required — data where data suffices, one shader where *motion* is required, two generated textures where *hand-painted irregularity* is required, and no Blender round-trip and no engine glow at all.

**Kept from lane 1 (Painted Flood):** the unshaded-and-pre-compensated principle; the `_wobble` rework from index-based (confirmed at `room_renderer.gd:1447`: `sin(i * 2.7)`) to **arc-length two-sine, no RNG** — with one correction: its `wobble_detail 0.22 m` is itself near-Nyquist against the 0.125 m post-Chaikin sample spacing (`smooth_iterations: 3`), so I set 0.5 m (≥4 samples/wave); the cascade **streak shader** concept (side fades so boxes stop reading as rectangles); the **generated circular-mark PNG** mechanism (dropped the billboard — moot once marks are flat); `Material.render_priority` for the near-coplanar layer stack; per-renderer capture calibration against the mockup hexes; the test ideas (foam width in px, material is self-lit, dims when dark).

**Kept from lane 2 (PAINTED WATER):** its pixel measurements, which drive my numbers — the foam's measured two-part profile (bright core + soft 8–10 px halo), the **measured ring set and sink-base centre** (my own arc-fit from the mockup puts the visible arcs at r ≈ 0.19/0.35/0.5/0.63/0.76 from the sink base — its tight spacing is right, lane 3's 0.42–1.20 m rings are not in the image), the yaw trick (same band, four rotations), the flat 6–12 cm white droplet marks, and `centre_z` as data; **alpha 0.85 + `dark_scale` off the existing `bulb_energy()`** (line 751 — this also inherits the death-beat flicker); the **`foam_strip` brush texture** concept with `rim_straddle` and `uv_per_metre`; the scoped `docs/06` amendment; and its transparency fallback (merge foam into the body mesh as a second surface) as my contingency.

**Kept from lane 3 (Second Light):** the decisive framing — **aim at the measured pixels, not the spec's hexes; the water is more *chromatic* than the floor, not more luminous; value-flatness is the signature**; the alpha arithmetic (at 0.45 the required blue needs **negative red** — impossible; 0.85 is the fix); its linear-space pre-grade derivation **#3B5880 = [0.231, 0.345, 0.502]**, which converges with my independent sRGB-space solve **[0.25, 0.35, 0.52]** — that convergence is why I can give one starting colour with confidence; rim/ring/whites as emission-grade values (translated to unshaded white); the idea of headless material assertions (`shading == UNSHADED`, `alpha ≥ 0.8`); and its honest list of what data alone cannot do (irregular foam, cascade streaking), which is exactly what lane 1's shader and lane 2's texture fill.

**Rejected, with reasons:** **engine glow** (lane 3's threshold-0.95 block) — it is a global frame effect the acceptance shot cannot sandbox; the frozen room's brightest pixel is the lamp shade at ~(253,253,248), which sits above the threshold and would start blooming, and on the phone's Compatibility path glow *shape* (`glow_strength`, `glow_levels`, blend mode) is a no-op, so the desktop review would not represent the phone. The mockup's soft halo is local to the water — the foam texture's feathered halo delivers it without touching the room. Also rejected: the **cool spill light** (the brief's own pillar is one bulb; the `light` block is skipped on `outline` regions today, `room_renderer.gd:1270`), the **WetHalo** additive band and the **near-sink wash** (no measured evidence they carry the read at phone size; each is another GL sorting risk and a day of work); **lane 2's five Blender meshes** and its five-texture bake (the heaviest pipeline and the largest new surface: 5 scripts → .glb → UV authoring, for a cascade, a ribbon, two splats and a sheen that a shader, a PlaneMesh and ~40 lines of vertex wobble cover at phone size); **billboarded dot droplets** (both lanes flagged StandardMaterial billboarding on GL as `[uncertain]` — a horizontally-oriented particle quad has no such question and matches the mockup's flat marks); lane 3's **emission-dominated *lit* body** (kept unshaded instead — it can never pick up the amber tint, and the darkness coupling is explicit via `dark_scale`); the `glow_fallback` per-renderer branch (nothing left to branch for); ring **travelling pulse**/animation (the mockup is a still; static is what was measured); and the `hazard_fade_s` pop fix (real, pre-existing, not one of the six features).

## 4. Feature-by-feature

1. **Puddle body.** Unshaded 0.85-alpha slate #3D5982 source → captures at the mockup's measured #485266 family; the 15 % floor show-through gives the measured ±5 % value-flatness for free (lit floor #7e573d vs its shadow both bleed ±0.15); it dims to 0.30 with the bulb, so darkness stays gameplay.
2. **Foam edge.** 0.085 m band, straddling 35 % inside, `foam_strip.png` at 0.9 tiles/m gives the measured bright core + soft halo + dash break-up; arc-length wobble (0.055 m, 0.9/0.5 m waves) supplies the large hand-painted lobes that the old index wobble could never make at any amplitude.
3. **Ripple rings.** Four white bands [0.18, 0.36, 0.55, 0.76] m centred on the **sink's base** (`centre_z 0.30`, matching the brief's own wording "around the sink's base" and my arc-fit of the mockup), 0.05 m wide, α 0.88·0.88ⁱ, radius-wobbled, four different yaws so the arcs stagger, drawn over the puddle at y 0.026.
4. **Cascade.** `water_fall.gdshader` on the jet and the three sheet boxes: pale-blue ground scrolling white foam streaks, side fades removing the box edges; `OverflowLip`, both rills and the foot are white unshaded (the foam hugging the cabinet face); the splash is 22 flat white marks bursting upward and falling (gravity corrected) — continuous with the rings by sharing the same white.
5. **Droplets/spray.** 24 flat white marks (`drop_mark.png` on horizontal quads, scale 0.08 ≈ 6–12 cm as measured), unshaded, α 0.95 — they can no longer read as shaded coins/pebbles because they no longer catch light or cast shading.
6. **Register.** Everything water is flat, unshaded and matte (specular 0.0/roughness 1.0 kept), white-on-slate against the warm floor — the one painted thing on a photographed floor. No glow, no grade change, no extra lights: the room renders exactly as frozen.

## 5. Renderer notes

Every mechanism is engine-level and listed as supported on both paths: unshaded `StandardMaterial3D`, alpha blend, textures with mipmaps, `Material.render_priority`, `ShaderMaterial` with `TIME`, `CPUParticles3D`. **Nothing exceeds the brief's ceilings.** Relative to the brief's list: no decals, no SSR/SSIL/SDFGI, no PCSS, no DOF, no debanding, no screen/depth texture, no CompositorEffect, no GPU particles, no new lights, no glow. Two device checks: `render_priority` ordering under the GL transparent sort — `[uncertain]`, low risk (documented material property on both renderers; the geometry y-ladder works independently, and the fallback is lane 2's merge-foam-into-body second surface); and texture alpha edges — if the phone shows dark fringing on the foam halo, the generator already writes halo RGB under transparent texels (premultiply-safe by construction). Off-screen effects of my plan on the frozen room: **none** — the only global values I touch are measured increments in the water's own materials; `post.saturation/contrast/brightness`, ambient, bulb and the environment are untouched.
